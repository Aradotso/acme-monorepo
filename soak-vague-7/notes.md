# Writing a Good Commit Message

A good commit message is concise, specific, and useful to someone reviewing the history later. Put the intent in a short imperative subject (usually no more than about 50–72 characters), then add a body when context, motivation, important trade-offs, or user-visible effects are not obvious from the subject. Describe what changed and why rather than narrating every implementation detail; use precise terms, consistent tense, and references to relevant issues when the project expects them.

Keep unrelated changes in separate commits, and do not hide breaking changes, migrations, compatibility constraints, or follow-up work. If a change reverts or fixes another commit, say so clearly and explain any consequences. Even for a one-line or mechanical change, make the subject accurate and meaningful; when there is genuinely nothing more to explain, omit the body rather than padding it.
